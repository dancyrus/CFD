Eight engines added to the preset library, together with three CEA-computed
propellant classes replacing the hand-entered handbook values that would
otherwise have backed them.

`docs/results/historical-presets-intel-xeon-4c.json`.

## 1. Propellant thermochemistry

Full table and method: `docs/results/propellant-cea.md`, generated by
`tools/propellant_cea.py`. Constant-enthalpy constant-pressure equilibrium
from the liquid reactants' heats of formation.

| engine | class | O/F | p₀ MPa | T₀ K | MW g/mol | γ_frozen | γ_eq | c*_frozen | c*_eq |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V-2 (A-4) | LoxEthanol75 | 1.130 | 1.57 | 2886 | 22.03 | 1.1947 | 1.1466 | 1612 | 1634 |
| Redstone | LoxEthanol75 | 1.324 | 2.19 | 3078 | 23.35 | 1.1876 | 1.1240 | 1620 | 1653 |
| Thor LR79 | LoxRp1 | 2.240 | 4.10 | 3491 | 21.93 | 1.2206 | 1.1479 | 1763 | 1802 |
| Atlas LR89 | LoxRp1 | 2.210 | 4.00 | 3472 | 21.79 | 1.2216 | 1.1496 | 1763 | 1802 |
| Atlas LR105 | LoxRp1 | 2.250 | 4.80 | 3514 | 22.01 | 1.2197 | 1.1485 | 1766 | 1805 |
| Titan I LR87 | LoxRp1 | 2.200 | 4.00 | 3466 | 21.75 | 1.2219 | 1.1503 | 1763 | 1802 |
| Titan I LR91 | LoxRp1 | 2.200 | 4.50 | 3479 | 21.77 | 1.2215 | 1.1514 | 1766 | 1803 |
| WAC Corporal | RfnaAnilineFurfuryl | 2.650 | 2.10 | 2987 | 25.62 | 1.2117 | 1.1515 | 1513 | 1538 |

**γ_frozen is what the presets carry.** The solver is a fixed-γ ideal gas with
no species transport, so the correct γ is cp/cv of the equilibrium chamber
mixture with its composition held fixed. CEA's shifting-equilibrium exponent
(γ_eq) is softer because it lets the gas recombine as it expands — physics this
solver cannot perform. The gap is 6% and would have shown up as over-predicted
exit Mach and thrust, so both columns are printed rather than one being quietly
chosen.

### Validation

Three independent checks, in increasing order of what they cover:

1. **The reactant cards.** LOX and RP-1 rebuilt from the stated enthalpies
   reproduce rocketcea's own library propellants to `5e-6` relative on T₀, c*
   and MW at two operating points. This is what says the element balance and
   the kJ/mol → cal/mol conversion are right.
2. **The equilibrium solve.** rocketcea (NASA CEA) against cantera/gri30 — a
   different code, different thermo database, different algorithm — agree to
   0.13% on T₀, 0.05% on MW, 0.01% on γ_frozen and 0.04% on c* across all eight
   cases. (gri30's NASA-7 polynomials are fitted to 3500 K and several cases sit
   at or just past it, so cantera is the cross-check, not the authority.)
3. **The acceptance gate**, below.

### Acceptance gate — Redstone

The one published throat dimension in the set:

```
throat dia 15.5 in            => A_t = 0.121736 m²
mdot 355 lb/s                 =  161.03 kg/s
p0  318 psia                  =  2.1925 MPa
delivered c* = p0·A_t/mdot    =  1658 m/s
```

| quantity | value | vs delivered | implied c* efficiency |
|---|---|---|---|
| ideal c*, frozen γ (solver-consistent) | 1620 m/s | −2.24% | 102.3% |
| ideal c*, CEA shifting equilibrium | 1653 m/s | −0.26% | 100.3% |

Both clear the ±8% gate. **But they clear it from the wrong side, and that is
worth stating rather than banking.** Delivered c* runs 92–96% of ideal in real
hardware; against these numbers it comes out above 100%, which no engine
achieves. The chemistry is not the suspect — see the three checks above. The
published trio is mutually optimistic instead. The most likely cause is that
318 psia is *injector-end* chamber pressure, which exceeds the nozzle
stagnation pressure by the Rayleigh loss across the combustor; a 3–5%
correction there restores a 96–99% efficiency and still clears the gate. Treat
1658 m/s as an upper bound on delivered c*, not a measurement.

### Why the presets do not use the class means

`PropellantClass::reference()` exists and holds the class mean, but a preset
carries its own case's numbers. The spread within a class decides this:

| class | cases | T₀ spread | MW spread | γ spread | c* spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LoxRp1 | 5 | 1.39% | 1.21% | 0.18% | 0.18% |
| LoxEthanol75 | 2 | 6.44% | 5.83% | 0.60% | 0.52% |
| RfnaAnilineFurfuryl | 1 | — | — | — | — |

One triple would be defensible for LOX/RP-1. It is not for LOX/ethanol: the V-2
and the Redstone sit at O/F 1.130 and 1.324, and using the mean would put both
engines ~3% wrong rather than either one right. `cases_span()` records the
measured min/max per class and `preset_gas_matches_its_propellant_class` holds
every preset inside its own class's range.

### The six earlier presets are NOT re-derived

They keep their per-engine researched values. The gap against CEA at each
engine's own operating point:

| preset | p₀, O/F | table T₀ / MW / γ | CEA T₀ / MW / γ_frozen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Merlin 1D | 9.7 MPa, 2.34 | 3600 K / 21.9 / 1.24 | 3643 K / 22.58 / 1.2146 |
| F-1 | 7.0 MPa, 2.27 | 3600 K / 21.9 / 1.24 | 3569 K / 22.19 / 1.2178 |

MW and γ differ by more than 1%. They were left alone for two reasons, both
specific. The F-1's γ is deliberately pinned at 1.24 so that its 3%-inside-the-
threshold separation warning at sea level reads as the design point it is
rather than as parameter drift — moving γ makes that warning flicker. And every
committed benchmark and results file in this repo was measured against these
numbers; re-deriving them is a change to the reference solutions, not a
correction to a preset, and belongs in its own work with its own re-run of the
ladder. `existing_six_presets_are_unchanged` pins all six field by field.

## 2. Throat radii: derived vs published

Only the Redstone's throat radius is a published dimension (15.5 in diameter →
196.85 mm; the preset carries 196.9 mm). The rest are derived. The derivation,
and what it gives back:

```
from thrust:    A_t = F_pub / (η_Cf · C_f · p0)        r_t = sqrt(A_t/π)
from mass flow: A_t = mdot_pub · η_c* · c*_ideal / p0
```

with C_f the quasi-1D ideal thrust coefficient at the engine's rating condition
(sea level, or vacuum for the two altitude-rated stages), η_Cf = 0.95 and
η_c* = 0.94 — mid-range handbook efficiencies, not fits.

| engine | C_f at rating | r_t from thrust | r_t from mdot | preset r_t | Δ vs thrust |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V-2 (A-4) | 1.361 sl | 197.6 mm | 196.0 mm | 206.0 mm | +4.3% |
| Redstone A-7 | 1.425 sl | 193.0 mm | 188.8 mm | **196.9 mm** (published) | +2.0% |
| Thor LR79 | 1.505 sl | 202.6 mm | — | 195.0 mm | −3.8% |
| Atlas LR89 | 1.500 sl | 202.5 mm | — | 170.0 mm | **−16.0%** |
| Atlas LR105 | 1.825 vac | 98.6 mm | — | 101.0 mm | +2.4% |
| Titan I LR87 | 1.500 sl | 193.0 mm | — | 190.0 mm | −1.6% |
| Titan I LR91 | 1.823 vac | 120.6 mm | — | 121.0 mm | +0.3% |
| WAC Corporal | 1.399 sl | 27.6 mm | — | 35.0 mm | **+26.8%** |

Published thrust and mass-flow figures other than the Redstone throat diameter
are commonly-cited values for 1950s hardware. They are a sanity check on the
derived geometry, not a reference this repo can stand behind.

### The three that miss by more than 10%, reported not tuned

The work order's constraint was to report a >10% mass-flow disagreement rather
than move r_t to close it, and three engines trip it. Moving a throat radius to
match a half-remembered thrust figure would launder a guess into a datum, so
none was moved. `derived_throat_radii_vs_published_performance` recomputes this
every run and fails if the outlier set changes.

| engine | derived mdot | published | Δ | derived thrust | published | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V-2 (A-4) | 138.1 kg/s | 125 kg/s | **+10.5%** | 270.5 kN | 249 kN | +8.7% |
| Redstone A-7 | 175.1 kg/s | 161.0 kg/s | +8.7% | 361.1 kN | 347 kN | +4.1% |
| Thor LR79 | 295.5 kg/s | — | — | 700.3 kN | 756 kN | −7.4% |
| Atlas LR89 | 219.1 kg/s | — | — | 517.4 kN | 734 kN | **−29.5%** |
| Atlas LR105 | 92.7 kg/s | — | — | 266.7 kN | 254 kN | +5.0% |
| Titan I LR87 | 273.7 kg/s | — | — | 646.2 kN | 667 kN | −3.1% |
| Titan I LR91 | 124.7 kg/s | — | — | 358.5 kN | 356 kN | +0.7% |
| WAC Corporal | 5.7 kg/s | — | — | 10.7 kN | 6.67 kN | **+61.0%** |

- **V-2**, +10.5% on mass flow, barely over. The 206 mm throat is ~4% larger
  than either derivation returns. Consistent with the Redstone's own >100%
  apparent c* efficiency, so the same injector-end-pressure suspicion applies —
  the V-2's 1.57 MPa is likely an injector-end figure too.
- **Atlas LR89**, −29.5% on thrust, the largest miss. 170 mm is far from the
  202.5 mm needed for 165,000 lbf at 4.00 MPa, and 202.5 mm is also what the
  same derivation returns for the Thor at nearly identical p₀ and ε — two
  engines of the same generation and family should not differ by 19% in throat
  radius at the same chamber pressure and area ratio. Either the r_t or the
  cited thrust is wrong; this cannot be settled from the figures on hand.
- **WAC Corporal**, +61% on thrust. Expected: its chamber pressure and area
  ratio are *reconstructed*, not published, so the reconstruction is what is
  being tested here and it does not close. 2.10 MPa on a 35 mm throat gives
  ~10.7 kN against the 1,500 lbf (6.67 kN) in the engine's own designation;
  either p₀ or r_t is too large by roughly a third. This is the least
  trustworthy preset in the library and its tooltip says so.

## 3. Separation at the default altitude

`default_altitude_km` exists because two of these engines are altitude-optimised
and genuinely separated at sea level. Quasi-1D p_e/p_a against the 0.40
Summerfield threshold:

| engine | M_e | p_e kPa | default alt | p_a there | **p_e/p_a at default** | p_e/p_a at 0 km |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V-2 (A-4) | 2.345 | 113.12 | 0 km | 101.33 kPa | 1.116 | 1.116 |
| Redstone A-7 | 2.525 | 112.71 | 0 km | 101.33 kPa | 1.112 | 1.112 |
| Thor LR79 | 3.174 | 65.63 | 0 km | 101.33 kPa | 0.648 | 0.648 |
| Atlas LR89 | 3.177 | 63.87 | 0 km | 101.33 kPa | 0.630 | 0.630 |
| Atlas LR105 | 4.006 | 17.03 | 25 km | 2.511 kPa | 6.784 | **0.168** |
| Titan I LR87 | 3.177 | 63.82 | 0 km | 101.33 kPa | 0.630 | 0.630 |
| Titan I LR91 | 4.014 | 15.87 | 58 km | 0.027 kPa | 593.3 | **0.157** |
| WAC Corporal | 2.806 | 65.35 | 0 km | 101.33 kPa | 0.645 | 0.645 |

All eight clear 0.40 at their own default altitude; the tightest is the Titan I
LR87 at 0.630. The two bold sea-level figures are why the field exists — opened
at 0 km, those presets raised a separation warning the instant they were
clicked, about nothing the user had done.

The test asserts this in **both** directions. Every zero-default preset must
*not* separate at sea level (or it would need a default of its own), and the two
non-zero-default presets *must* separate at sea level (or the non-zero default
would be decoration). A field that only ever gets checked in the passing
direction can be all zeros and still pass.

The Titan I LR91's design altitude is 60 km, above the model's 58 km atmosphere
and slider ceiling, so `default_altitude_m()` clamps it to 58 km. `atmosphere()`
clamps at the same altitude, so the ambient is the one the model has anyway; the
clamp only keeps the slider handle on its track. Past that end of the slider sits
the app's labelled vacuum stop, which a preset does not select on the user's
behalf.

## 4. Run time to convergence

Measured, not projected: throughput over 250 steps after a 50-step warm-up, then
the case run to the §9 visual-steady step count with the wall clock taken.
(The residual-based `converged` flag is not a usable finish line here for the
same reason it is not for the demo case — an overexpanded sea-level plume keeps
breathing above the 1e-3 threshold indefinitely.)

| engine | ε | grid | cells | steps/s | steps to steady | **wall time** | × Merlin 1D |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V-2 (A-4) | 2.83 | 105 × 188 | 19,740 | 288.1 | 4,668 | **18.2 s** | 0.26 |
| Redstone A-7 | 3.61 | 113 × 188 | 21,244 | 275.1 | 4,983 | **20.9 s** | 0.30 |
| WAC Corporal | 5.0 | 124 × 201 | 24,924 | 242.5 | 5,469 | **25.3 s** | 0.38 |
| Titan I LR87 | 8.0 | 144 × 234 | 33,696 | 198.0 | 6,325 | **36.2 s** | 0.60 |
| Thor LR79 | 8.0 | 144 × 234 | 33,696 | 194.4 | 6,325 | **36.3 s** | 0.60 |
| Atlas LR89 | 8.0 | 144 × 234 | 33,696 | 194.4 | 6,325 | **36.9 s** | 0.60 |
| Titan I LR91 | 25.0 | 218 × 325 | 70,850 | 101.4 | 9,466 | **95.7 s** | 1.88 |
| Atlas LR105 | 25.0 | 218 × 325 | 70,850 | 101.8 | 9,466 | **98.0 s** | 1.88 |

As expected, the cheap end of the library: 18–98 s against the demo case's Large
domain at 804 s and Merlin Vac's ~12× Merlin 1D. Short nozzles buy small domains
as well as short ones, because `preset_domain` sizes off ε.

Run-to-run spread on this machine is about 2% (an earlier pass of the same
benchmark read 17.9–96.4 s), so treat the last digit as noise. The cell counts
and step counts are exact — they are geometry, not timing.

### Positivity floors

Four presets touch the positivity floor during the cold-start front and are
silent afterwards — the §13 quarantine class, same shape as the existing
presets. Steady-state floor contact would be a hard failure (cumulative
activations blank every readout under the product rule), so the benchmark
asserts the last activation lands in the first half of the run.

| engine | activations | last at step | of |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thor LR79 | 1 | 98 | 6,325 |
| WAC Corporal | 4,861 | 132 | 5,469 |
| Atlas LR105 | 6,868 | 206 | 9,466 |
| Titan I LR91 | 9,781 | 343 | 9,466 |

The two altitude-optimised presets start into a very thin ambient (25 km and
58 km), which is where the startup blast expands hardest before relief. The
other four presets never touch the floor at all.

### Solved mass flow vs quasi-1D

Recorded because it is uniform and therefore not a preset problem:

| engine | solved mdot | quasi-1D ideal | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| V-2 (A-4) | 118.7 kg/s | 129.8 kg/s | −8.6% |
| Redstone A-7 | 150.2 kg/s | 164.6 kg/s | −8.8% |
| Thor LR79 | 252.6 kg/s | 277.8 kg/s | −9.1% |
| Atlas LR89 | 187.4 kg/s | 206.0 kg/s | −9.0% |
| Atlas LR105 | 79.4 kg/s | 87.1 kg/s | −8.9% |
| Titan I LR87 | 234.0 kg/s | 257.2 kg/s | −9.0% |
| Titan I LR91 | 107.1 kg/s | 117.2 kg/s | −8.6% |
| WAC Corporal | 4.9 kg/s | 5.3 kg/s | −8.7% |

The deficit is −8.6% to −9.1% across eight engines spanning a factor of 25 in
mass flow, three propellants and two altitudes. That is an engine-independent
property of the grid at 20 cells/r_t — the §8 1/N_throat systematic error the
report's own N_throat badge exists to flag — not a verdict on any preset's
throat radius. Worth noting where it lands: it moves the V-2's solved mass flow
to 118.7 kg/s against the published 125 kg/s, closer than the +10.5% quasi-1D
derivation that flagged it as an outlier in §2. The flag stands on the quasi-1D
figure, which is the one independent of this solver's discretization.

## 5. Redstone throat area from the rasterized wall

The whole geometry chain — spec, contour generator, polygon clipper — measured
against the one published dimension. Open cross-sectional area per axial column
from the exact solid fractions, minimised over columns:

| measurement | A_t | vs published 0.121766 m² |
|---|---|---|
| published (15.5 in dia) | 0.121766 m² | — |
| preset r_t, geometric | 0.121798 m² | +0.027% |
| rasterized, 80 cells/r_t | 0.121847 m² | +0.067% |
| rasterized, production 20 cells/r_t | 0.122151 m² | +0.316% |

Both rasterized figures clear the 0.5% tolerance and both are asserted. The
production grid reads high for a stated second-order reason: a column integrates
the wall over its own width and r(z) is quadratic at a throat, so the cell-mean
area exceeds the minimum by ≈(dz²/12)/R_arc — about 0.3% at dz = 0.145 r_t on
the §10 family's 0.382 r_t downstream arc. It falls as dz², which the refined
figure confirms (0.316% → 0.067% at 4× resolution).

## Open items

1. **Atlas LR89 throat radius.** −29.5% on thrust, and inconsistent with the
   Thor at nearly the same p₀ and ε. Needs a real source for either the throat
   dimension or the rated thrust.
2. **WAC Corporal reconstruction.** p₀ and ε are reconstructed and do not
   reproduce the engine's own 1,500 lbf designation. Needs published figures,
   which were not found.
3. **V-2 area ratio.** 2.83 per Sutton; museum-hardware dimensions elsewhere
   imply ~3.34. Unresolved, and carried in the preset's tooltip.
4. **The Redstone c* efficiency above 100%.** The gate passes, but the published
   throat area, mass flow and chamber pressure cannot all three be right. Most
   likely 318 psia is injector-end pressure.
5. **The six earlier presets' gas properties** differ from CEA at their own
   operating points by more than 1% in MW and γ. Deliberately not changed here —
   see §1.
